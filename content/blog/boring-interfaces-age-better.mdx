Most long-lived systems are held together by interfaces that are almost disappointing. A request in, a result out, and very little ceremony between the two.

## Novelty spends a maintenance budget

An interface becomes a permanent promise much faster than the implementation behind it. Every optional flag, abstract factory, and clever naming scheme ends up teaching someone what to expect later.

The better question is not “what might this need to do?” It is “what can this promise without regret?”

> A boring boundary gives the inside room to change.

## Fewer concepts, sharper ownership

Small interfaces make ownership visible. The caller owns intent. The callee owns execution. If a boundary needs to negotiate too much context, it is usually crossing the wrong seam.

That restraint is not a lack of ambition. It is how you preserve it.
